01 · Plan

Where the RL curriculum stands, and what's next

Stages A through F already run end to end: a PPO curriculum that tracks a reference motion, holds its centre of mass over the support polygon, manages momentum, absorbs falls, recovers from them, and switches between those modes on a threshold rule. Stages G, H and I below are the open plan — the work that turns a working pipeline into a research result.

02 · Walkthrough

How the work got here

Two pipelines share one motion library: the research track trains a robot to survive being a martial artist, the film track shows one performing like one.

research pipeline
1

Source footage: Kalaripayattu clips from Kerala Tourism and Kalari Warriors, one motion per clip.

2

GEM-X retargets the captured human pose onto the Unitree G1's skeleton — SAM-3D-Body, then SOMA, then a soma-retargeter pass. Five install defects in that chain were found and fixed along the way.

3

annotate_motion_library.py writes each clip to data/motions_retargeted/*.npz and tags it into one of four families — stable stance, translational, explosive strike, rotational — in configs/motion_families.yaml.

4

Stages A–F train against that library in order: tracking, then centre of mass, then momentum, then fall, then recovery, then the mode switch.

5

sim_push_sweep.py audits the trained switch with lateral pelvis pushes, 0–100N, from a horse stance — the seed Stage H grows into a full curriculum.

6

Every reward and every evaluation number reads centre of mass, capture point and support polygon from one place — src/dynamics/pinocchio_wrapper.py — nothing recomputes that math twice.

arena film pipeline

The same retargeted library also feeds a cinematic track: the G1 corps performing a set of moves inside an arena built from five real Indian monuments under Creative Commons licence — Charminar, Qutub Minar, the Gateway of India, the Taj Mahal, and the Indian flag. Early cloud-GPU 4K runs kept dying at scene-build, so the arena now renders end to end on a local Blender pipeline, with the cloud box reserved only for re-export. That pipeline has produced a full 4K cut and five shorter edits, all in the outputs section below.

03 · Novelty

What makes this a research result, not just a pipeline

Stage G

Evolving the transitions nobody captured

No clip in the library goes from ky_warrior_lunge to pk_kick_lunge — that transition was never filmed. Stage G fills gaps like it with a genetic algorithm over a 5–7 keyframe, 29-DOF chromosome, seeded from the two real neighbouring clips rather than random noise, and scored on the same stability primitives — com_support_margin, capture_point_margin — the RL reward already computes. It runs in low single-digit minutes on CPU per transition, because filling in a keyframe gap is a static trajectory-optimisation problem, not a sequential decision one. RL stays reserved for what it's actually good at.

Stage H

Training for a push, not just measuring one

sim_push_sweep.py already answers whether a trained policy survives a lateral push — but only as a one-off audit, after training is done. Stage H turns that into a curriculum the policy trains against directly: full 360-degree direction, a randomised contact point on the pelvis, torso or a limb to emulate a strike or an obstacle rather than a clean push, and a force ramp gated on the policy's own success rate. That is the gap between evaluating robustness and training for it.

Stage I · stretch

A model that predicts the hit before it lands

A forward-dynamics model trained on the rollout logs every stage already writes, predicting the next joint state and centre of mass from the current one, the action taken, and the estimated external force. This is the piece the plan itself flags as what turns the project from an engineering pipeline into a research result — held as a stretch goal deliberately, so it never blocks demoing Stages G and H on their own.

what's actually been measured so far

The 49 trials currently in results_paper/trials.csv ran against a scripted PD plus threshold-switch controller — not yet the trained Stage A–F policies now in the codebase. Closing that gap is what Stage G and H are for. On that scripted baseline: zero falls up to 100N of lateral force, certain falls at 120N and above. In a 420N impact test, the same controller kept the torso in contact 5 of 5 times against an explicitly protective variant's 2 of 5 — but head force measured zero in both arms, so that is not evidence of head protection, and isn't claimed as any. The capture-point margin threshold used in training, δ₁ = 0.015, was corrected down from the 0.05 written in switch.yaml after measuring the real horse-stance margin with true foot geometry (+0.026m) — the smaller, honest number is what everything downstream uses.
